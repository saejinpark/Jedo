## Description

Jedo is a hand-drafting desk laid on your iPad. Real-size rulers, set squares, a compass and templates are held with your fingers, and Apple Pencil draws along them like a pencil. It is not CAD: there is no dimension entry, no automatic shapes and no zoom. A centimetre on screen is a centimetre on paper, so your hand learns the size of things.

You don't need to know it all first. Tap ? in the corner, then tap any button, and it tells you what that tool is and how it is used.

In the hand
• Drafting pencils 0.3, 0.5, 0.7 and 0.9 mm and a wood pencil, in eleven grades from 4H to 6B.
• Technical pens 0.13–1.0 mm, and the G and maru dip nibs of manga. A nib's line swells as you press.
• Three erasers (rubber, putty, electric) and an erasing shield: rub over it and only what shows through its openings is erased.
• A compass: plant the needle with a finger and swing the pencil round it.
• A knife, a stylus and a roller.

On the desk
• Parallel rule, straight ruler, 45° and 30°/60° set squares, triangular scale (1:100–1:600), protractor
• Circle, ellipse, square and hexagon templates, a lettering guide, a braille slate and a Morse plate
• Three French curves and a curve rule for clothing patterns
• Perspective pins: rest a ruler against a pin and every line runs to the vanishing point.
• A tape measure, in centimetres or inches: spread two fingers and it opens between them.
• Tools work together. Slide a set square along the parallel rule; hang a ruler on a pin.

Paper and sheets
• A4–A0 and B5–B1 paper, or fit to screen. The paper lies on the desk at its real size.
• Tracing paper, yellow trace and graph tracing paper to lay over the drawing, move and turn. Stamp a finished tracing down onto the sheet below.
• Manuscript paper, manga paper, a squared exercise book, handwriting practice paper, music staff paper and a four-line notebook.
• Screen tone: lay it, cut it with the knife, and scrape light out of it with the blade laid flat.
• Carbon paper in plain colours, gradients (mix your own from up to four colours) and cloth prints (stripe, gingham, polka dot, tartan, houndstooth). Cut it to a garment's shape and roll over it to print that shape in the cloth.
• Photos and PDF pages laid like prints: trace them, cut them out and glue them down for collage, or turn them face down and use them as carbon.
• The knife parts a sheet where it is drawn across, and cuts out a shape where it is taken round.

And
• Right- and left-handed arrangements. Groups of buttons can be moved where you like and stood upright.
• The drawing saves itself and exports as a PNG.
• Turn the iPad and the drawing stays under your hand.

Made for students of architectural and engineering drawing, for people who draw perspectives and manuscripts by hand, for pattern drafting and fashion sketches, and for anyone who wants to keep the feel of the drawing board on an iPad.

I am not a drafting professional, so there is surely much that falls short. I would be grateful for feedback from people who really draft, draw manga or cut patterns: where a tool looks or works differently from the real one, which tools are missing, where it doesn't fit your hand. I will keep fixing it.

No personal data is collected. Your drawings and the photos you choose stay on your iPad.
